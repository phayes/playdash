1. Dynamic segment production for live streams
----------------------------------------------

Goal: play `MPD@type="dynamic"` manifests, where segments appear over time and
old ones fall out of the time-shift window, without changing how static
manifests behave. The list of media segments stops being a fixed `Vec` that
`DashManifest::segments` builds once; `Segments` produces media segments on
demand instead.

Decisions (settled)

- `Segments` wraps a closed `MediaIndex` enum (`List`, `Template`,
  `Timeline`), not a trait object: callers don't supply their own sources.
- `Segments` no longer needs to be serialisable; drop `Serialize` /
  `Deserialize`.
- Walking the index (a cursor whose step yields a segment, "not before
  <time>", or the end) is internal to the schedule, not public API.
- One refresh per presentation, shared by every stream opened from the same
  `DashManifest`, run on demand by whichever stream's worker needs it
  (Phase 4).
- Media segments are identified by a sequential number per representation,
  kept aligned by start time when an index refreshes, as ExoPlayer does
  (Phase 2, alignment in Phase 4). The next segment is always number + 1.
- Seeks outside the available window clamp into it. Falling behind the window
  while reading follows a policy chosen on the builder (Phase 5).

Prior art (dash.js 8502c40, androidx/media 8c6678b)

- ExoPlayer `DashSegmentIndex` (exoplayer_dash): an immutable, pure index
  queried with `now` passed in: `getSegmentNum(time)`, `getTimeUs(n)`,
  `getFirstAvailableSegmentNum(now)`, `getAvailableSegmentCount(now)`,
  `getNextSegmentAvailableTimeUs(now)`, and `getSegmentCount`, which is
  `INDEX_UNBOUNDED` for live templates. Template availability is arithmetic,
  in `SegmentBase.MultiSegmentBase`.
- ExoPlayer refresh: `DashMediaSource` owns one manifest load per
  presentation and pushes each new manifest through
  `DashMediaPeriod.updateManifest` to every track's `DefaultDashChunkSource`.
  `RepresentationHolder.copyWithNewRepresentation` keeps a `segmentNumShift`,
  since numbers drift across refreshes, and throws `BehindLiveWindowException`
  on a gap. Stale manifests (older `publishTime`, fewer periods) are retried,
  then fail with `DashManifestStaleException`. `minimumUpdatePeriod = 0`
  falls back to 5 s. `UTCTiming` sets a client clock offset.
- dash.js: stateless getters per addressing (`TemplateSegmentsGetter`,
  `TimelineSegmentsGetter`, `ListSegmentsGetter`). One `ManifestUpdater` per
  player refreshes on a `minimumUpdatePeriod` timer, paused while playback is
  paused. `DashHandler` continues from `lastSegment`'s presentation time, so
  refreshes need no renumbering. Behind the window, `PlaybackController` seeks
  to the window start (to the live edge in low-latency mode); seeks are
  clamped to the window.
- Segment identity: ExoPlayer's `MediaChunk.chunkIndex` is the segment number
  plus `segmentNumShift`, the next chunk is `chunkIndex + 1`
  (`DefaultDashChunkSource.getSegmentNum`), and behind the window is
  `segmentNum < firstAvailableSegmentNum`. Its loaded queue
  (`ChunkSampleStream.mediaChunks`) is trimmed from the front, and positions
  in it are never the identity. dash.js steps by `index` for templates and by
  time for timelines, and tracks loaded requests by `startTime`
  (`FragmentModel`).

What blocks it today (Phases 1 and 2 removed the parallel media/timeline
lists and positional segment identity)

- `Schedule` assumes the stream has an end: `complete`, `past_end`,
  `total_len`, `SeekFrom::End`, the symphonia-compat length estimate, and
  `BufferTotal`.
- Manifest timing (`manifest/segments.rs`) needs a period end or `@endNumber`
  to bound `S@r="-1"` and `@duration` addressing, and fails with
  `DashManifestMissingDuration` without one. Live bounds them by the wall
  clock instead.
- `@type`, `availabilityStartTime`, `minimumUpdatePeriod`,
  `timeShiftBufferDepth`, `suggestedPresentationDelay`, `publishTime` and
  `availabilityTimeOffset` are parsed by the vendored dash_mpd_core but unused.

Plan, in phases that each ship on their own

Phase 1 (done): one list for media and timing
- `Segments` holds a private `MediaIndex` (`Untimed(Vec<SegmentLocation>)`, or
  `List` of `MediaSegment { location, start, duration }` on a `MediaTiming`
  clock) with accessors; `MediaTimeline` is gone, and `SegmentStream::new*`
  take only `Segments`. Every reported time is presentation time (entry 2,
  folded in). `SegmentList` and `SegmentBase` honour
  `@presentationTimeOffset`.

Phase 2 (done): stable segment identity
- `SegmentNumber`, a crate-internal sequential number per representation (the
  position, for static segments), identifies media everywhere in a stream:
  `Part::Media(SegmentNumber)`, the retention window, `PartBody`, the
  demuxer's `Loaded` and `PacketStream` seeks.
- The schedule keeps its media in a `VecDeque<Scheduled { data, span }>`
  numbered from a base `first`, so Phase 4 can append at the back and drop
  from the front without renumbering. The stream's `first` offset arithmetic
  is gone; the cursor carries `first` to step from the init to the first
  media segment.

Phase 3: a segment index behind `Segments`
- `Segments` already wraps a crate-internal `MediaIndex` (`Untimed`,
  `List`) behind accessors. Add the live variants:
      enum MediaIndex { Untimed(..), List { .. }, Template(..), Timeline(..) }
  each defining segment numbers, and share it as `Arc<MediaIndex>`.
  `Segments::new(init, media)` and manifests' static segments behave
  exactly as today.
- Index queries are pure and take `now`, modelled on ExoPlayer's
  `DashSegmentIndex`: the segment at a media time, the segment after a given
  start, the first available segment and the live edge at `now`, the end
  (`None` while live), and when the next segment becomes available.
- The schedule materialises segments from the index around the cursor as the
  window moves, through the internal cursor. It holds an `Arc<MediaIndex>`
  snapshot and swaps it when a refresh lands (Phase 4). The streams run all
  I/O; the index and `Schedule` stay free of it.
- Segments the schedule adds after it opens need their decryption roles set
  (`Store::assign_media_roles`), which today happens once, at open.

Phase 4: the live index and the shared refresh
- Parse and keep the dynamic-MPD fields listed above in `DashManifest`
  (an `is_live()` and the timing anchors).
- `Template` index (`$Number$` + `@duration`): availability is arithmetic, and
  finding new segments needs no MPD refresh. Segment `n` is available once
  `availabilityStartTime + Period@start + (n - startNumber + 1) * duration -
  availabilityTimeOffset` has passed (its end, less the offset); the earliest
  available segment comes from `timeShiftBufferDepth`.
- `Timeline` index: `S` entries merged by start time on each refresh, and
  entries older than the time-shift window dropped. Segment numbers stay
  aligned across the merge by matching start times, as ExoPlayer's
  `segmentNumShift` does; an old index ending before the new one starts is
  the behind-the-window case of Phase 5.
- `BufferHandle` takes its segments and spans from the schedule instead of a
  snapshot at construction, once the schedule's segments change while a
  stream reads.
- Shared refresh: a dynamic `DashManifest` holds its live state behind an
  `Arc`, so every stream opened from it (audio and video) shares one refresh:
      struct Live {
          url: Url,
          state: RwLock<LiveState>,  // parsed MPD, a MediaIndex per representation, fetched_at, publish_time
          generation: AtomicU64,
          refreshing: ..,            // one fetch at a time: blocking and async locks, as SegmentData has
      }
  `DashManifest` loses its derived `PartialEq` and `Default`.
- Refreshes run on demand, with no timer thread. When a stream's GET worker
  reaches the live edge and a refresh is due (`fetched_at +
  minimumUpdatePeriod`, or a `Timeline` index has run out), it takes
  `refreshing`. If `generation` moved while it waited, it picks up the new
  index; otherwise it fetches the MPD through its `Transport`, rebuilds every
  representation's index, and bumps `generation`. Refreshing stops when no
  stream is pulling. `Template` indexes still refresh at
  `minimumUpdatePeriod`, only to learn of an end, a new period, or a switch to
  static.
- The MPD fetch goes through `Transport` (today `new_from_url` calls ureq
  directly), keeping the final URL after redirects and honouring `Location`.
- A refresh whose `publishTime` is older than the current one is stale: retry
  a bounded number of times, then fail. `minimumUpdatePeriod = 0` refreshes at
  a fallback interval (ExoPlayer uses 5 s); a dynamic MPD without
  `minimumUpdatePeriod` does not change and is never refetched.
- Start position: `suggestedPresentationDelay` behind the live edge (or a
  caller-chosen delay), not time zero.
- Inject a clock (`trait Clock { fn now(&self) -> SystemTime }`) so tests can
  be deterministic. Leave room for a server offset from `UTCTiming`, a
  follow-up: both players rely on it, and live playback breaks under client
  clock skew.
- `MPD@type` switching from dynamic to static on a refresh ends the stream
  cleanly.

Phase 5: stream semantics at the live edge
- `Read` and the iterator wait for the next segment's availability instead of
  returning EOF; EOF only once the source reports an end.
- Byte offsets: byte 0 is the start of the init, then the first segment the
  stream joined at. `prefix_len` only covers segments seen since joining, so
  `SeekFrom::Start` works only within the materialised range and
  `SeekFrom::End` errors while live. symphonia-compat length estimates are not
  available live.
- `seek_time_coarse` (and so `seek_time` and `open_at`) clamps a time outside
  the available window into it, as dash.js does, and reports where it landed
  through the returned position.
- Behind-window policy, chosen per stream on the builder:
      pub enum Jump { WindowStart, LiveEdge }
      MediaStreamBuilder::behind_window(self, jump: Jump) -> Self   // blocking and async
      SegmentStream::with_behind_window(self, jump: Jump) -> Self
  Stored as `Option<Jump>`. Unset, reading fails with a recoverable
  `Error::BehindLiveWindow { available: Range<Duration> }`; the stream stays
  usable and the caller seeks back into the window. `Jump::WindowStart`
  continues from the earliest available segment (dash.js's default);
  `Jump::LiveEdge` from the live edge less the start delay (dash.js's
  low-latency mode). No effect on static manifests.
- The policy applies at the first media segment the stream never fetched that
  has left the window: segments already downloaded stay readable after the
  server drops them. A gap between an old and a refreshed index (ExoPlayer's
  `BehindLiveWindowException` case) is the same event.
- A jump shows as a forward `tfdt` discontinuity in the byte stream and a
  timestamp gap in packets. Check that symphonia's isomp4 demuxer accepts a
  `tfdt` jump before relying on jumps through the byte-stream path.
- Retention `behind` defaults to the time-shift window depth, so memory stays
  bounded even with `Retention::default()`.
- `BufferStatus.total` becomes optional (or reports the available window) while
  live.
- Rodio `DashSource`: `total_duration` is `None` while live, and seeks clamp
  to the available window.

Out of scope for this entry (note, don't solve)
- Multi-period live, where a new period can bring a new init segment.
  `SegmentNumber` should leave room for a period index; the byte stream would
  need to re-emit an init at the boundary, which most fMP4 demuxers only
  accept by starting again.
- Low-latency DASH (chunked transfer, `availabilityTimeComplete=false`); it
  ties into the existing TODO in schedule.rs about publishing a `moof` before
  its `mdat` arrives. A low-latency media segment arrives as several CMAF
  chunks, each a `moof`+`mdat` movie fragment, so this is where "fragment"
  (or "chunk") names a part of a segment.
- Adaptive bitrate switching.
- Refreshes signalled in-band by `emsg` (ExoPlayer's `PlayerEmsgHandler`), and
  MPD patches.

Open questions
- `Segments` accessors for a live index: what does a caller see in place of
  today's `media` list (the segments available now, or nothing)?
- `behind_window(Jump)` with the recoverable error as the unset default, or a
  three-variant enum (`WindowStart`, `LiveEdge`, `RecoverableError`) that can
  name the default explicitly? `Jump` at the crate root may also want a more
  specific name, such as `LiveJump`.


2. Presentation time: timeline origins and decoder timestamps
------------------------------------------------------------

Done with entry 1's Phase 1: every reported time is presentation time,
`Period@start + (media time - @presentationTimeOffset) / timescale`, and
`MediaTiming::presentation_time` maps a decoder's media timestamps onto it.
A time before the first segment lands on the first. `MediaTimeline` is gone.

Left to do
- Change the video_player example to time frames by decoder timestamps
  (ffmpeg can print them with `-vf showinfo` or `-copyts`) instead of
  counting frames from the segment start.


3. Container wrapping for raw-demuxer decoders
----------------------------------------------

Goal: every codec's `MediaStream` output can be piped to a decoder that reads a
byte stream, such as the `ffmpeg` CLI through ffmpeg-sidecar, without the
caller writing a container muxer.

What is missing today

- Opus has no raw byte-stream form: `ffmpeg` and other raw demuxers read it
  only in Ogg, with the `OpusHead` and an `OpusTags` header first and the
  packets paginated, each page with a granule position and CRC.
- VP9 has none either: it needs IVF, a 32-byte file header (width, height,
  timebase) and a 12-byte frame header before each frame.
- AAC has `DecoderFormat::AacAdts`, but only when asked for by format; neither
  `DecoderStyle` gives it.
- tests/decoder.rs already wraps all three for its ffmpeg checks (`feed`, with
  Ogg pages and CRC, IVF, and ADTS); none of it is public.

Plan

- Promote the Ogg Opus and IVF wrapping from tests/decoder.rs, either as new
  `DecoderFormat`s (e.g. `OpusOgg`, `Vp9Ivf`) whose initialization is the
  container header and whose packets carry their page or frame header, or as
  separate helpers that wrap a `MediaStream`'s initialization and packets.
- Update `DecoderStyle`'s decoder table: the ffmpeg-sidecar Opus and VP9 rows
  point at the new format.

Open questions
- New `DecoderFormat`s, or helpers outside the format enum? Formats keep "one
  packet in, one packet out" and work with `MediaStreamBuilder::format`, but
  they are containers, not decoder input.
- Ogg granule positions and IVF frame counts after a seek: restart the
  container, or keep counting?
- IVF's header has a frame count, unknown while streaming: write 0, as ffmpeg
  accepts?
